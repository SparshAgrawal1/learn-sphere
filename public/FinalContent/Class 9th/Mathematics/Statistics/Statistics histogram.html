📖 Theory & Key Concepts

📊

What is a Histogram?

A histogram is a graphical representation of the distribution of continuous numerical data. Unlike bar graphs, histograms show how data is distributed across continuous intervals or ranges.
  • Bars touch each other (no gaps)
  • X-axis represents continuous ranges
  • Y-axis shows frequency or count
  • Used for continuous data only
🔢

Frequency Density

When class intervals have different widths, we use frequency density instead of raw frequency to ensure fair representation.
  • Formula: Frequency Density = Frequency ÷ Class Width
  • Ensures area of bar represents frequency
  • Prevents misleading visual comparisons
  • Essential for varying class widths
⚖️

Histogram vs Bar Graph

Understanding the key differences helps you choose the right visualization for your data.
  • Data Type: Continuous vs Categorical
  • Bars: Touching vs Separated
  • Purpose: Distribution vs Comparison
  • X-axis: Ranges vs Categories
🎯

Construction Steps

Follow these steps to create accurate and meaningful histograms:
  • 1. Draw horizontal and vertical axes
  • 2. Choose appropriate scales
  • 3. Draw bars with equal widths
  • 4. Ensure bars touch each other
  • 5. Add title and labels
