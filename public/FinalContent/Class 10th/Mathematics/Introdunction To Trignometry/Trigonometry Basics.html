📐 What is Trigonometry?

Trigonometry is the branch of mathematics that studies the relationships between the angles and sides of triangles. It's the mathematical tool that helps us solve problems involving triangular shapes and circular motion.

Triangle + Measurement = Trigonometry
Ancient wisdom meets modern precision
🌍 Historical Origins

Born in ancient Egypt and Babylon around 3000 years ago, trigonometry helped early astronomers track celestial bodies and architects build magnificent structures like pyramids and temples.

Ancient Wisdom → Modern Technology
From pyramid builders to rocket scientists
🔬 Modern Applications

Today, trigonometry powers GPS navigation, computer graphics, sound engineering, medical imaging, robotics, and space exploration. It's literally everywhere in our digital world!

Smartphones to Spacecraft
Hidden mathematical engine of technology
📐 Right Triangle Foundation

The right triangle (with one 90° angle) is the cornerstone of trigonometry. It has three sides: hypotenuse (longest), opposite (across from angle), and adjacent (next to angle).

90° Corner = Trigonometry Gold Mine
Where all ratios come to life

📚 Historical Timeline of Trigonometry

3000 BC

Ancient Babylonians

First recorded use of trigonometric concepts for astronomical calculations and land surveying.

2700 BC

Egyptian Pyramids

Trigonometric principles used in the precise construction of the Great Pyramid of Giza.

300 BC

Greek Mathematics

Hipparchus creates the first trigonometric table and develops systematic trigonometry.

150 AD

Ptolemy's Almagest

Comprehensive astronomical work featuring advanced trigonometric calculations.

1600s

Modern Trigonometry

Isaac Newton and others develop calculus-based trigonometry for physics and engineering.
